## Observatory orientation study — 2026-08-19

A new standalone study explores the Observatory as the public, low-commitment
interface through which several CSMCL.SPACE surfaces become perceptible:

`../experiments/observatory-orientation/observatory-orientation-001.html`

It currently presents:

- **Arrival** as observable now, linking to the active local TAIC Territory
  Arrival moodboard rather than the deployed legacy `/arrival/` route;
- **Firmament** as an open field, linking to the existing Firmament subdomain;
- **Nexus** as a distant informational coordinate with no entry action;
- **Innerstellar** as a latent informational possibility with no vessel or
  identity assigned;
- **Oracle** named quietly at the outer range, without a page or interface;
- **Zee** as Traveller Trace 001, linking to the existing field archive and
  explicitly framed as one travelled example rather than a map of the whole.

The first composition was corrected after review because its large declarative
title and four prominent information panels blocked the territory and completed
too much of the visitor's message. The current version therefore:

- uses only `CSMCL.SPACE · Observatory` as its title;
- uses the same complete-plate framing established by the Arrival moodboard,
  preserving the foreground tower/platform and the far-right territory at the
  same time;
- lets the territory remain the primary communication, with no interface over
  its geographic centre;
- places title and field status in the quiet upper band and coordinates plus
  the Zee trace in the lower band;
- reduces the four surfaces to a thin coordinate rail;
- reports only near, open, distant, and latent conditions;
- keeps explanatory fragments extremely faint until local attention reaches a
  coordinate.

This is a communication invariant, not merely a styling preference: the canvas
must create the visitor's question, and interface language should provide
orientation without closing interpretation.

The standalone study remains the visual provenance for the integrated root.
Its Firmament link returned HTTP 200 when checked on 2026-08-19. The composition
was visually checked at 1920×1080, 1366×768, and a narrow single-column layout
before promotion. The eventual institutional `csmcl.space` root remains a
separate, unsettled communication problem.
