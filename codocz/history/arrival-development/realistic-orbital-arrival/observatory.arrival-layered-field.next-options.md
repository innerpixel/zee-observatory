# Observatory exterior :: next options from the layered field

*The place continues. A vessel can be played with. The rail makes selected
relations more perceptible.*

## Why this document exists

The exterior image is now materially separated into:

```text
OBSERVATORY BASE
  station · Earth · starfield · no independent vessels

ARRIVING VESSEL
  transparent pod layer · independently positioned · able to move later

INTERFACE
  observation rail · current packets and controls
```

This separation creates several credible directions. The next step should be
chosen by discussing what kind of attention and play the field should support,
not by promoting another small animation into the meaning of the exterior.

“Arrival” remains an internal route and production term. It is an implied
state, not something the public experience needs to declare or describe.

## Present understanding

The Observatory is already here and has independent activity. The vessel gives
the visitor a situated, potentially playable presence without making the world
depend on them. The rail can sample what becomes perceptible from the general
field or from a localized area.

```text
OBSERVATORY
  independently living place

VESSEL
  optional movement · position · proximity · play

RAIL
  recurring observation pattern · local resolution · implied vocabulary
```

The visitor completes their own experience from what is present. No authored
sequence needs to tell them what conclusion to reach.

## Invariants shared by every option

- Station activity continues independently of the vessel and pointer.
- Moving, hovering, or focusing changes local perception—not world causality.
- The general field remains available when nothing is being localized.
- No action is required for the place to feel complete.
- Missing one observation is not failure; recurrence creates another opening.
- A report must remain readable and must not interrupt a report already held.
- Repeated words gain meaning through visible relations before explanation.
- The rail does not diagnose, greet, recruit, or infer identity.
- Remaining, exploring, approaching, and leaving remain legitimate.
- The station must still feel whole if every overlay is removed.

## The rail pattern worth retaining

The failed timed carrier cut contained one useful interaction form:

```text
LOCAL FIELD
  quiet cadence / moving points

RESOLUTION
  a source or condition begins becoming distinguishable

FIELD OBSERVATION
  one restrained report resolves

HOLD
  enough uninterrupted time to read and compare

RETURN
  report recedes · general field remains
```

The value lies in recurrence. The first cycle may be peripheral. A later cycle
allows the visitor to recognize a pattern and decide to watch more closely.
The rail teaches its behaviour without a tutorial.

The retained static build of this experiment lives at:

`experiments/observatory-arrival/retained-studies/experiential-cut-001-static-build/`

## What recurring observations could gradually imply

No individual packet needs to explain the scene. Together, packets may make a
working vocabulary perceptible:

```text
GENERAL FIELD
  local movement · distance · orbit · traffic · unresolved source

VESSELS
  carrier · holding · vector · destination · passing · remaining

STATION
  broadcast · receiver · local condition · structure · route

DOCKING
  approach · berth · occupied · available · clearance · departure
```

Meaning emerges through recurrence and relation. “Holding” becomes legible
because a vessel remains outside the station. “Berth” becomes legible because
the same station region changes while vessels arrive or depart. “Local
broadcast” becomes legible because it resolves more clearly near the station.

These are candidate vocabulary families, not approved public terminology.

## Option 1 — passive recurring field

The vessel hovers independently but is not controllable yet. The rail cycles
through a small, carefully authored sequence of general observations. Visual
activity and rail reports share the same world, but do not require exact
synchronization.

### What it studies

- whether recurrence itself creates curiosity;
- whether several fragments begin mapping the scene;
- whether the rail feels alive rather than promotional;
- whether quiet remains complete between reports.

### Strength

Smallest content study. It isolates rail rhythm and vocabulary before adding
input complexity.

### Risk

The visitor may read the rail as a passive slideshow. The independently layered
vessel may look playable without actually responding.

## Option 2 — pointer as attention lens

The general field continues while the pointer is in open space. Resting the
pointer over a meaningful area gradually tunes the rail toward that region.

```text
OPEN SPACE
  general field continues

VESSEL AREA
  vessel-local conditions become clearer

STATION BODY
  station-local observations resolve

DOCKING ARM
  approach and berth relations become perceptible
```

Pointer movement does not immediately replace text. A short dwell indicates
attention; the current report completes; the next report may come from the
localized source. Leaving the region returns gradually to the general field.

### What it studies

- whether spatial attention makes the image more investigable;
- whether the rail reads as a receiver rather than a tooltip;
- whether localized vocabulary becomes understandable through context.

### Strength

Directly connects scenery and rail without requiring vessel controls.

### Risk

Rapid hover changes could become noisy. Mouse-only discovery would exclude
touch and keyboard unless equivalent focus behaviour is designed.

## Option 3 — arriving vessel as movable receiver

The visitor may gently reposition the pod. The vessel's location changes what
the rail can resolve, while Observatory activity continues independently.

```text
far from station
  broad field · weak or unresolved local sources

near station
  station broadcasts and structural conditions resolve

near docking region
  berth and approach relations become locally legible

withdraw
  localized observations soften · general field returns
```

The vessel does not collect, score, unlock, or activate station events. It
changes vantage and reception.

### What it studies

- whether the instinct to play with the pod is real;
- whether movement feels exploratory rather than task-like;
- whether proximity can teach the field's vocabulary;
- whether the visitor understands that they reveal rather than cause events.

### Strength

Most embodied and playful option. It gives the independent vessel layer a
meaningful role.

### Risk

Control behaviour may dominate attention and turn the station into scenery.
Poorly staged responses may imply that the world waits for the vessel.

## Option 4 — gradual hybrid

The exterior begins as Option 1: a complete independently active field with a
recurring general rail pattern. Without instruction, the visitor may discover
that attention over scenery localizes later observations. Vessel movement, if
introduced, deepens spatial reception rather than replacing pointer attention.

Possible progression of discovery:

```text
general field is observable
  → rail recurrence becomes recognizable
  → attention localizes a source
  → vessel position changes reception range
  → the general field always remains available
```

This is not a public progression ladder. Nothing is unlocked as a reward; the
instrument simply becomes more legible through use.

### What it studies

- the complete relationship among world, vessel, attention, and rail;
- whether passive observation and play can coexist;
- whether the interface supports different depths without a guided sequence.

### Strength

Closest to the broader understanding: independent world, optional play,
proportionate response, and vocabulary acquired through experience.

### Risk

Too many behaviours at once may make cause and effect ambiguous. It should be
approached through separate paper and interaction studies rather than built in
one pass.

## Comparison

| Option | Primary experience | Main question | Complexity | Principal risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Passive recurring field | Watch and gradually recognize | Does recurrence map the scene? | Low | Feels like a slideshow |
| Pointer attention lens | Look toward a region | Can attention localize the rail? | Low–medium | Tooltip-like or mouse-dependent |
| Movable vessel receiver | Explore through position | Does vessel play create meaningful reception? | Medium | Control overwhelms observation |
| Gradual hybrid | Observe, discover, then play | Can all three remain coherent? | High if built at once | Causal ambiguity |

## The key unresolved input choice

Pointer location and vessel position should not silently mean the same thing.
We need to decide:

```text
POINTER AS GAZE
  what part of the scenery am I attending to?

VESSEL AS POSITION
  from where is the local field being received?
```

If both are present, the rail may need to distinguish attention from reception
without exposing an elaborate ontology. For example, vessel position could
determine available sources while pointer attention selects among those that
are already locally perceptible.

That relation is promising but should not be assumed before testing each input
separately.

## Smallest discussion artifacts before more code

### Artifact A — spatial field map

Mark four broad regions on the existing image without public labels:

```text
open field
arriving vessel
station body
docking region
```

For each region, record what is directly visible and what may become more
locally perceptible. If a region has nothing meaningful to contribute, it does
not need interactive treatment.

### Artifact B — observation packet set

Write:

- 5–7 general-field observations;
- 3 vessel-local observations;
- 3 station-local observations;
- 3 docking-region observations.

Read them as a group and ask whether they imply one coherent place without
explaining it.

### Artifact C — rail cadence storyboard

Show only five static rail frames:

```text
quiet → gathering → resolved report → held report → quiet
```

Test readability and attention without canvas animation.

### Artifact D — separate input probes

Prototype cursor dwell and vessel movement separately. Do not combine them
until each has a perceptually clear effect.

## Decisions for discussion

1. Is the first playful discovery looking toward scenery, moving the vessel,
   or either?
2. Is the rail attached perceptually to the vessel, to the Observatory, or to
   the exterior field between them?
3. Does the vessel move freely, follow gently, drag directly, or choose among
   broad positions?
4. Which station regions already carry enough visible meaning to localize?
5. Should general observations continue while a local source is held, or queue
   until attention returns to open space?
6. How long should a resolved report remain, and may an important report recur?
7. What is the minimum vocabulary needed before a docking relation becomes
   inferable?
8. What equivalent attentional behaviour works for touch, keyboard, and reduced
   motion?

## Recommended next option to study

Study **Option 4 as a direction, but not as one implementation**.

Begin with Artifacts A and B: the spatial field map and observation packet set.
Then test the repeating rail cadence without requiring synchronized canvas
events. After that, probe pointer localization and vessel movement separately.

This order preserves the larger idea while letting each relationship earn its
place:

```text
what is here
  → what may be observed
  → what recurrence makes recognizable
  → what attention localizes
  → what movement makes newly perceptible
```

The next code change should follow the first paper artifact that produces a
clear, meaningful difference—not precede it.
