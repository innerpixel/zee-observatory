# Observatory station story and vantage study 001

*Before deciding how the visitor attends, decide what is genuinely there to
notice.*

## The correction this study makes

We have developed a substantial interaction grammar: recurrence, optional
attention, local resolution, a quiet rail, a movable vessel, and a world that
does not wait for the visitor. The exterior still needs a stronger content
centre.

The next question is not initially:

> What should the interface do?

It is:

> What ongoing life and relations should become observable in this scenery?

The story does not need to be a narrated sequence. It may be the visitor's
gradual recognition of several processes that already coexist.

## Proposed whole impression

This is the internal content intention, not public wording:

> A lived-in Observatory listens beyond the visible field while coordinating
> local movement around it. The nearby vessel is one relation within that
> field, not the cause or centre of it.

The visitor should not be told that an arrival has occurred. “Arrival” remains
an internal route name and an implied spatial condition. What the visitor can
encounter is simply what is here.

The whole impression can be carried by three ongoing station stories:

```text
HABITATION
  the structure is lived in and tended

RECEPTION
  the structure listens and exchanges beyond the visible frame

COORDINATION
  vessels, routes, docks, and local processes continue around it
```

None of these is a plot event. Together they make the Observatory a place.

## What exactly may become observable

| Thread | Direct visual evidence | Question that may form | Rail contribution: one fact at most | What remains unresolved |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Habitation | Uneven warm windows, living growth, one slow internal light change | Who or what lives here, and what continues inside? | `inhabited ring · cycle unchanged` | Occupants, purpose, and interior life |
| Reception | Dishes or antennae adjust independently; a restrained local pulse has a source | What is the station listening to? | `receiver cluster · orientation changing` | Sender, content, distance, and whether anything will answer |
| Coordination | A dock changes condition, a distant craft holds or passes, another berth remains occupied | Is there a procedure or pattern to the traffic? | `outer berth · occupied` | Rules, destinations, authority, and why a craft remains |

The rail phrases above describe content density; they are not approved public
copy. Each packet should confirm only a visible relation. It should not explain
the Observatory or name the visitor's situation.

## Spatial content map

The existing exterior can support a small vocabulary if each region has a
reason to be observed.

### Open field

May contain distant motion, an unresolved route, or a weak carrier that
continues beyond the frame.

What it contributes: the visible scene is not the boundary of the world.

### Station body and dome

May show nonuniform habitation lights, vegetation, reflections, and internal
cycles that do not respond to the visitor.

What it contributes: this is a lived place, not an interface object.

### Receiver crown

May show antenna orientation, dish relation, or a pulse whose origin and
destination remain unknown.

What it contributes: observation and exchange are actual station processes.

### Docking arms

May show occupied, quiet, clearing, receiving, or maintenance conditions.

What it contributes: local movement has structure without requiring that its
procedure be explained.

### Nearby vessel

May hover, drift slightly, or change reception through position. It should not
be the source of every visible event.

What it contributes: the visitor has a situated relation to the field while
remaining free to watch, play, or leave.

## One coherent exterior, not three unrelated animations

The three threads should overlap as ambient life rather than queue as chapters.
A visitor may first notice any one of them. Recurrence lets the other relations
become available later.

```text
station remains inhabited
  + receiver activity occasionally resolves
  + traffic conditions occasionally change
  = a place continuing on its own
```

The rail samples this life. It does not manufacture it. A visual condition
should exist long enough to create a question before a rail packet confirms
one restrained fact about it.

## Different angles and parallax are two different tools

They should not be combined accidentally.

### Parallax inside the current view

Parallax requires layers that share one perspective:

```text
far starfield
  Earth and atmospheric horizon
    Observatory station
      nearby vessel
        very restrained foreground signal or reflection
```

Small relative movement can create depth while preserving the scene. This can
be tested with the empty background plate, station extraction, and vessel
extraction once the station matte and reconstructed background are clean enough.
The present base plate still contains the station and therefore cannot sit
under a moving station layer without revealing a duplicate.

### Movement between different viewpoints

Generated angles represent different camera positions. They are candidates for
discrete vantages, a later route around the station, or references for a proper
multi-view asset. Fading or morphing among the present studies would make the
station's architecture visibly mutate.

If continuous movement around the station becomes important, first create an
identity sheet that fixes:

- number and placement of principal arms;
- docking-port shapes;
- dome and ring proportions;
- receiver-crown arrangement;
- underside silhouette;
- lighting and vegetation rules.

A small 3D station or genuinely consistent multi-view production would then be
more reliable than unrelated generated rotations.

## Smallest content study worth making next

Remain in the present exterior view. Do not add all three angles yet.

Prepare one static field map and one small content set:

1. one habitation condition on the station body;
2. one receiver condition near the crown;
3. one traffic or docking condition near an arm;
4. one restrained rail fact for each condition;
5. quiet recurrence with enough separation that each visual relation can be
   noticed before any text appears.

This is enough to test whether several small observations accumulate into a
whole place. Pointer localization and vessel movement can later reveal these
same relations at greater resolution; they do not need separate stories.

## Fresh-viewer evidence we want

Without being asked to decode lore, a fresh viewer should be able to form some
version of at least two of these impressions:

- the station is inhabited or tended;
- it is listening, receiving, or communicating with something unseen;
- docking or traffic follows an ongoing local pattern;
- activity is not waiting for or produced by the visitor;
- there is more beyond the current frame than can presently be resolved.

Their interpretation does not need to match ours. The useful evidence is that
they construct a relation from visible details and can say what made them
think it.

The study is weakened if viewers notice only a beautiful spaceship, treat the
rail as a changing caption, assume every activity is a message addressed to
them, or believe the station is waiting for them to trigger the next event.

## Open decision

Which whole impression should lead the first exterior study?

```text
THE OBSERVATORY IS LIVED IN
THE OBSERVATORY IS LISTENING
THE OBSERVATORY COORDINATES MOVEMENT
```

All three may eventually coexist. Choosing which one should become noticeable
first will determine the first meaningful visual evidence and prevent the next
prototype from being driven by interface behaviour alone.

Working recommendation: let **habitation** be continuously present in the base
scene, let **reception** provide the first transient observation, and let
**coordination** become visible through later recurrence. These are overlapping
conditions, not chapters, and the visitor may notice them in another order.
