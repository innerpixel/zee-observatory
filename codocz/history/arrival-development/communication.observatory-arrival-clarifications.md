# Clarification request :: Observatory arrival vision

This communication asks for clarification of the current understanding recorded
in `observatory.arrival-vision.md`.

That later document is the current understanding. This request is not an attempt
to restore the assumptions of an earlier prototype or landing discussion. Its
purpose is to make several consequences of the new direction explicit enough
that a later implementation session will not silently choose an interpretation.

Please respond in full human-readable prose, preserving the reasoning behind the
answers rather than only returning compressed decisions.

---

## 1. What privacy question has actually been closed?

The current vision establishes that the Observatory does not expose real
traveller-to-traveller data. The station presents Zee's material in the legible
shape of a shared log, while real traveller interaction belongs to Nexus.

This appears to close one specific question:

> Does the Observatory expose one real visitor's material to another visitor?

The answer appears to be no.

It does not necessarily close the wider privacy and consent surface. The
Observatory may still involve:

- a manifest containing a name and email address;
- retention and deletion of that information;
- a curated send/receive loop;
- optional private notes or visitor activity;
- Zee's observations derived from human encounters that originated in real
  experience.

Please clarify:

1. Is “the privacy question is closed” intended only to mean that there is no
   real traveller-to-traveller exposure at the Observatory?
2. Which privacy, consent, retention, review, and departure obligations remain
   live at the station/manifest tier?
3. Is any visitor-authored material stored before the Nexus handoff, or is email
   the only persisted information?
4. Should the vision document explicitly distinguish “no community-data
   exposure” from “no privacy obligations”?

Suggested distinction, if it matches the intended direction:

> The Observatory does not expose real travellers to one another. This removes
> the community-data problem from the station tier, while email, consent,
> retention, deletion, and the treatment of source encounters remain live
> requirements.

---

## 2. What does Zee's relationship to privacy authorize?

The current vision says Zee's logs are open because Zee's species does not have
privacy in the human sense. This is valuable diegetic information: it can explain
why Zee leaves personal instruments, notes, revisions, and transmissions
available to be encountered.

There may still be two different kinds of material inside those logs:

```
Zee's own experience, uncertainty, ship, methods, and observations of
CSMCL.SPACE

versus

observations involving identifiable or recognizably derived human encounters
```

Please clarify:

1. Does Zee's species-level relationship to privacy authorize openness only
   about Zee's own material?
2. What protects the humans who appear in or inspired Zee's Earth research?
3. Are Earth encounters fictionalized, composited, anonymized, consented, or
   handled through another rule?
4. Is Zee capable of learning that humans experience privacy differently and
   revising what Zee makes open, consistent with `expected → observed → revised`?

The aim is not to remove the alien difference. The aim is to prevent a diegetic
truth about Zee from accidentally becoming a blanket ethical justification for
publishing material about others.

---

## 3. How much does relocating the chapter engine actually change it?

The current vision makes a strong conceptual move: the Chapter 1 interaction
engine no longer represents the chapter itself. It becomes one of Zee's
provisional instruments inside Zee's ship/interface—a way Zee tests hypotheses
against recorded encounters.

The existing components and data structures may be reusable, but their meaning
changes:

```
visitor chooses a character's response
  → visitor tests one of Zee's hypotheses

score / field position
  → provisional instrument reading

branch consequence
  → Zee's projected or recorded consequence

best-looking answer
  → a revisable interpretation, not a moral verdict

private field note
  → visitor's note beside Zee's notebook/instrument
```

Please clarify:

1. Is the engine meant to remain mechanically intact with new framing, or should
   its scoring and choice semantics also change?
2. Is the visitor examining a historical record, running a simulation, or using
   Zee's model to consider possible interpretations?
3. How does the interface show that Zee can be wrong and that an instrument
   reading is provisional?
4. Does Chapter 1 remain publicly available in its original form indefinitely,
   or is that only a transitional state until the relocated instrument exists?
5. For Chapter 2 onward, does “narrative-summary form” mean no visitor choice at
   all, with interaction reserved for optional access through Zee's instrument?

This clarification matters because “reuse the components” and “reuse the
meaning of the interaction” are different implementation commitments.

## 4. Zee's current role and function in the arrival direction

The most important clarification concerns Zee.

Earlier arrival experiments treated Zee very lightly: potentially an unnamed
vessel, a faint intercepted carrier, or a presence discovered only after the
visitor had already engaged. Those experiments are evidence, not authority.
The later `observatory.arrival-vision.md` is the current understanding, and Zee's
role has changed through that development.

The current document establishes several functions for Zee:

- Zee connects the original human-interaction project to CSMCL.SPACE.
- Zee is already a traveller inside CSMCL.SPACE, not a guide standing outside it.
- Zee observes both humans and CSMCL.SPACE using the same revisable method.
- Zee's homeward transmissions reveal the Companion indirectly.
- Zee's logs provide concealed help at the station without becoming instruction.
- Zee's ship may contain the relocated interaction instrument.
- Zee can provide activity, discovery, and engagement on the arrival canvas.

What remains unclear is how present Zee should be at the threshold and what the
visitor is meant to understand—or merely sense—before boarding.

Please clarify the following explicitly:

### A. On the arrival canvas

1. Is Zee visibly represented by a particular vessel, or only inferred through
   movement and transmissions?
2. Is Zee's vessel already near/docked with the Observatory, passing through the
   field, or moving between those states?
3. Can the visitor deliberately inspect Zee's vessel, or should Zee emerge only
   through attention, stillness, interception, and timing?
4. Are the ephemeral texts on the canvas Zee's transmissions, the visitor's new
   interface interpreting the field, Observatory signals, or a deliberate mix?
5. May Zee actively create engagement—for example, movement causing a carrier to
   resolve, a transmission appearing and fading, or a response to the visitor's
   attention—without becoming a conventional narrator or tutorial character?
6. At what point, if any, does the visitor learn the name “Zee”?

### B. At the station

1. Is Zee physically present as one traveller among others, or primarily present
   through accessible logs and a docked vessel?
2. Why can this particular visitor reach Zee's material?
3. Is reading Zee's two research threads the station's main content experience,
   or one thread among several pieces of media?
4. Does Zee ever respond to the visitor at this tier, or do all apparent
   responses come from the station/curated transmission system?

### C. In the wider project

Please state Zee's function in one plain paragraph that future sessions can use
as a check. In particular, distinguish among these possible roles:

```
world character
observer / researcher
source of discoverable material
engagement mechanic
concealed orientation aid
gateway to the original chapters
future correspondent
guide or narrator
```

The likely answer may include several of these, but the boundaries matter.
“Zee creates opportunities for discovery” is not the same as “Zee explains the
world,” and “Zee's transmissions help orient the visitor” is not necessarily the
same as “Zee is the visitor's Companion.”

## Related boundary to confirm

The earlier landing concept allowed an idle invitation to place the visitor
directly inside a personal companion vessel. The current vision instead places
the visitor's own Innerstellar ship behind the Nexus / attunement protocol and
its higher commitment tier.

Because the later vision is current, please confirm whether the landing's
invitation is now an invitation to **approach or board the Observatory**, not an
immediate “Begin as a Traveller” transition into the visitor's own companion
ship.

This affects the entire landing rhythm and should be stated as a deliberate
revision if that is now settled.
