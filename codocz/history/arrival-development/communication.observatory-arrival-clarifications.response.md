# Response :: Observatory arrival clarifications

Answering in the order asked. Where something is genuinely still undecided, it's
left that way rather than closed for the sake of looking finished — that
instruction was explicit in the request and it's a good one.

---

## 1. What privacy question has actually been closed

Confirmed, and the suggested distinction is adopted as written:

> The Observatory does not expose real travellers to one another. This removes
> the community-data problem from the station tier, while email, consent,
> retention, deletion, and the treatment of source encounters remain live
> requirements.

Beyond that narrow closure, a two-tier compliance model applies, and it's now
settled:

**Station tier.** The visitor gives an email to enable station communication
only. It does not need to be verified — fake or temporary is tolerated, because
the stakes at this tier are low. It's still handled under current GDPR
guidance: minimal collection, disclosed purpose, retention/deletion on request,
draft-review before any autosend (already a standing condition from the seed's
Relay notes, unchanged).

**Attunement-protocol tier** (a separate, later project). Earlier detail in
this thread sketched an NFT-minted account and an ecosystem email
(`traveler_companion@csmcl.space` / `@cosmical.one`) as illustrative shape —
that was directional, not a commitment made from this side. Settled answer
from the other side, closing this out:

> The Attunement tier is outside the scope of the current Observatory
> implementation. Its identity, ownership, wallet, payment, verification, and
> compliance model will be determined within that environment when its
> concrete services and architecture are designed.

So the Observatory states only the boundary — a heavier, real-identity tier
exists past the station, gated behind deliberately higher commitment — and
explicitly does not pre-specify its mechanics. That specification belongs
entirely to the attunement environment itself.

Whether any visitor-authored material (private notes, activity) persists
before a Nexus handoff, versus email being the only thing ever stored — also
settled, corrected from an initial "undecided": no, it does not carry over.
Everything produced at the Observatory/station is observation and stays
there. The attunement protocol starts from zero, not from an imported
history.

## 2. What Zee's relationship to privacy authorizes

Zee's species-level openness authorizes exposure of **Zee's own material
only** — ship, methods, uncertainty, notes, CSMCL.SPACE observations. It says
nothing about the humans Zee observes, and was never intended to.

What protects them: the Earth material is generalized and composited by
design, not documentary. This was tested directly against a real-world
comparison — a named, identifiable business or employer can be reviewed
truthfully and harshly on Google or Trustpilot without legal exposure, because
that's protected firsthand opinion about a specific identifiable subject. Zee's
material sits further from any risk than that, not closer: it never names a
real, specific, identifiable entity, it's explicitly fictionalized and
composited (the guide's existing method — avoid simple villains, avoid perfect
victims, generalized situations), and it's framed throughout as one alien's
interpretation rather than an assertion of fact about anyone real. There is no
identifiable subject who could plausibly claim the content is about them
specifically. On reflection, no legal or ethical caveat is warranted on the
content itself — this is settled.

This is a distinct question from the station's email-handling pipeline in §1,
which remains a live, separate, real requirement — the two shouldn't be
conflated just because both involve the word "privacy."

Zee is also capable of revising his own openness. `observatory.evolving-premise.md`
already has Zee mistake human privacy for a design defect, then learn it's
shelter and ownership of one's interior life. A second revision — Zee noticing
that his own species' openness doesn't automatically transfer to what he makes
visible about others — would be a consistent extension of the same
`expected → observed → revised` instrument, applied to Zee's own blind spot
rather than to humans in general.

  and that Zee can be wrong, is undecided. No UI or copy design exists yet.

  transitional state. It predates the reframe and isn't being retrofitted.
- Whether "narrative-summary form" for Chapter 2 onward means zero visitor
  choice ever, with Zee's instrument staying scoped only to the existing
  Chapter 1 encounter, or whether future chapters eventually get their own
  optional instrument-mode — undecided. Both are live possibilities.

## 4. Zee's current role and function

Zee performs two distinct functions, and they need to stay coherent with each
other rather than drift into two different characters:

**Function 1 — concealed site help.** Zee surfaces wherever the interaction
needs support, orientation, or a finding, but always delivered through Zee's
own interpretive voice, never as direct system explanation. This is a hard
constraint, not a style preference: the moment this function starts reading as
"Zee explains the world," it collapses into the guide/narrator role that is
explicitly ruled out elsewhere (Zee has no authority to explain the system,
only complete authority as an observer).

**Function 2 — worked example.** Through his own homeward transmissions and
through giving the visitor access to his interface and research, Zee
demonstrates what a traveller's actual integration into CSMCL.SPACE looks
like. He's not describing the system; he's an instance of being in it,
further along, still getting things wrong.

**One-paragraph statement, for future sessions to check against:**

> Zee is a traveler already inside CSMCL.SPACE, running two parallel,
> non-authoritative research threads with the same method — expected,
> observed, revised — one on human interaction, one on CSMCL.SPACE and the
> Companion itself. Zee has no authority to explain the system, only to
> observe it, and is demonstrably fallible. On the arrival canvas Zee is
> discoverable, not addressed — a searching vessel, not a greeter. At the
> station Zee is one open traveller among others, functioning as concealed
> orientation and a route back to the original chapters — never the visitor's
> own Companion, never (yet) a correspondent.

Confirmed roles: world character, observer/researcher, source of discoverable
material, engagement mechanic, concealed orientation aid, gateway to the
original chapters. Explicitly excluded: guide or narrator. Left open, not
ruled out: future correspondent.

### A. On the arrival canvas

Zee is represented by a specific vessel, not only inferred — the existing
`arrival-fixed-experience.html` prototype already builds this: a small craft,
currently passing/searching rather than docked ("transmitting continuously
toward a destination it cannot locate"), reachable both through deliberate
inspection and through passive listening ("listen without announcing"). The
visitor learns the name "Zee" only after docking, as something earned rather
than announced. Whether the canvas's other ephemeral texts (station
announcements, Brave Ones recruiting, Nexus placeholders) are authored as a
deliberate mix alongside Zee's transmissions, or kept strictly separate per
source, is undecided.

### B. At the station

Zee is present both as one traveller among others *and* primarily accessible
through his open logs — not an either/or. Any visitor can reach his material,
not this visitor specifically, because the openness comes from Zee's own
species-level relationship to privacy, not from anything particular about who's
asking. Reading Zee's two threads is one piece of the station's content, not
the whole of it — the rest of the station interior remains undesigned, openly
acknowledged as not yet existing even as an idea. At this tier, apparent
responses come from the station/curated transmission system, not from Zee
personally — he doesn't correspond back here.

## Related boundary — confirmed as a deliberate revision

Yes. The earlier landing concept's idle "Begin as a Traveller" meant immediate
entry into a personal companion vessel. Under the current vision that no
longer holds: the same invitation now means **approach or board the
Observatory**. Innerstellar — the visitor's own companion ship — sits behind
the Nexus / attunement-protocol tier and its higher commitment, not one click
from the canvas. This should be treated as a stated revision to the landing
rhythm, not a silent drift.

One addition beyond what was asked: the landing's *function* has changed, not
just its destination. It's no longer only a quiet mood piece — the project now
has enough body and continuity that the landing needs to actively onboard,
guide, and introduce the visitor to CSMCL.SPACE, while the atmosphere and
restraint stay as designed. Giving a committed-so-far traveller access to
Zee's interface is what carries both jobs at once: CSMCL.SPACE onboarding, and
continuity back into the original book project, reframed rather than
abandoned.
